## 0. The supervisor's decisions (override the proposal below)

1. G17–G23 and the G2 amendment approved in principle, **except G22**: the orchestrator may
   run GPU jobs up to a stated time limit, always after a committed smoke test; longer or
   remote jobs go to the supervisor as exact commands.
2. Default **3 seeds** per configuration (G18).
3. Local GPU allowed within that limit. The limits are stated per project in
   `docs/COMPUTE.md` (template defaults: CPU 10 min, GPU 30 min per job; the supervisor edits them).
4. **Method checklists** (skills `deep-learning`, `connectivity`, `compute-jobs`), not new agents.
5. Tools: grouped splits with the leakage check, unit-level permutation and chance level
   are **core** (`eegharness.splits`, `.permutation`, `.chance`, numpy only).
   Deep-learning modules are an **optional dependency**: `eegharness.seeds` and
   `eegharness.folds` never require torch (torch is used only if the project installs it).
   FDR/NBS were not approved for the kit: projects implement them with the tests the
   `connectivity` checklist requires.
6. The derived-number check (G23) is the **auditor's**, not the guard's: a results check
   on `06_results.md`, recorded in `05_audit.md`.

**L28 folded in** (P300 SSVEP-methods port, plan audits P0–P1, [7] L28):
- into **G17**: any transform applied to test samples must be computable without their
  labels (L28 d: subtracting the class-mean response needs the test trial's label);

- into **G19**: development data (anything used to build, tune or debug) never enter the

## 2. Proposed general rules (G17–G23)

Each rule gets an *Incident* line, as G9–G16 have. For these rules the incidents come
from the SSVEP project, so this is the first time the harness takes lessons from a second study.

### G17. Training stays inside the training data (deep learning and pooled participants)

Extends G1. Proposed text:

> Every fitted step of a network's training uses training units only (the G13 unit):
> (a) the early-stopping / validation split is drawn from the training fold, grouped by the
> same unit, never from the test fold or a held-out participant;
> (b) normalization statistics come from the training fold, or are computed per sample
> without statistics across samples (stated which);
> (c) augmentation runs on the fly, on training samples only, and never mixes classes
> (class-conditional mixup only);
> (d) hyperparameters, architecture, epochs and checkpoints are chosen on inner validation,
> never on test results;
> (e) unsupervised test-time normalization (statistics from unlabeled test data) is allowed
> only when labelled as such in the method name and the write-up, with the source of the
> statistics stated (whole session = offline upper bound; first K units = online);
> (f) when participants are pooled, group ids are unique across participants, all
> participants share one channel set (chosen by name), and nothing from the held-out
> participant reaches any fit;
> (g) operations over consecutive samples (smoothing, context windows, sequence models)
> never cross a unit boundary, and samples are ordered by recording time, not by label or
> file order.

Sources: R2 [1:43]; golden rule 6 [5 §2.6]; PF-29 augmentation scope, PF-32 early stopping
on test data, PF-35 transductive normalization, PF-33 id collisions, PF-34 channel sets,
PF-02 / R8 smoothing across trials [1:74], PF-36 trial order [2]; P10 task 1 and P23
`fit_gnn` [3:368, 3:867]. *To open:* Varoquaux 2018 (CV failure with small samples);
Roy et al. 2019 (DL-EEG review, reporting practices).

### G18. Seeds and spread

> Every stochastic model is seeded per fold and repeat before the model is built, DataLoader
> generators included, and the seed scheme is written in the plan. Every reported number of
> a stochastic model is the mean and SD over at least N seeds (N in the plan, default 3),
> per participant, with the value of each seed saved. A/B comparisons use the same seeds and
> folds (paired). A difference smaller than the seed spread is reported as "no difference
> shown". Final training accuracy on un-augmented training data is reported next to test
> accuracy, and the parameter count next to the architecture. Device and library
> nondeterminism (GPU, cuDNN flags, library versions) go into `provenance.json`.

Sources: PF-28 nondeterministic A/B, PF-30 train accuracy diagnostic [2]; the NN tolerance
of ±5 pp per subject from seed and GPU nondeterminism [6, tolerance table]; P10 "same seeds",
P15 seeds `1000·r + fold`, P23 `--seeds 3` [3]. In the opposite direction, P20 ran the NN
window sweep with `--cnn-repeats 1` [3:753]: one seed, no spread. *To open:* Bouthillier et
al. 2021 (variance in ML benchmarks).

### G19. Significance budget for model comparisons

> Before results, the plan states the comparisons that may be called significant: the
> primary contrast(s) (e.g. network vs the classical baseline), the test and its unit
> (participant-level paired test, or a permutation over the G13 unit), α, and the correction
> across comparisons. **Every configuration tried counts against the budget**: architectures,
> variants, sweeps, seeds chosen and epochs. Comparisons outside the budget are reported as
> exploratory and never starred. Accuracy is compared with a chance level that accounts for
> the number of test trials (not only 1/k). A network that wins on the mean but not per
> participant is reported per participant.

Sources: [own]. The SSVEP project had no such budget. Its NN sweep, its 9 feature sets ×
2 k values in the connectivity gate, and its 2 variants × 2 normalizations in the GNN were
compared descriptively [3 P15 task 3, P20, P23]. The gate verdict was explicitly a
"descriptive heuristic" [3:578–579]. Related: G10 (frozen before data), PF-20 (separate
criteria). *To open:* Combrisson & Jerbi 2015 (chance levels with small samples);
Demšar 2006 (comparing classifiers); Cawley & Talbot 2010 (selection bias in model
evaluation).

### G20. Connectivity estimators and their statistics

> (a) **Estimator bias.** Every connectivity estimator has a known-answer test on independent
> noise that reports its bias at the sample size used. Conditions are compared at matched
> sample sizes, or estimated once per condition over concatenated data; per-sample
> estimates of a biased estimator are never averaged.
> (b) **Phase.** Phase is taken by a method valid for the segment length, with an
> edge-immunity test: corrupting the edge samples barely changes the estimate.
> (c) **Baseline.** Stimulus vs baseline uses the same band and the same estimator.
> (d) **Permutation.** Units are permuted (G13) with the observed group sizes. The number of
> permutations can resolve α (p ≥ 1/(n+1)). A result that crosses α between two permutation
> counts is inconclusive.
> (e) **Criteria.** Global, edge-wise (FDR) and component-wise (NBS) criteria are reported
> separately, never merged into one flag. The FDR level, NBS threshold and component measure
> are fixed before results (G10); an NBS threshold sweep is reported as a sensitivity
> analysis, not used for selection.
> (f) **Directed measures** are symmetrized (estimate and null alike) before undirected
> statistics.
> (g) **Missing channels** are embedded by name with NaN before averaging across
> participants.
> (h) **Subgroups** (e.g. responders) are defined independently of the measure under test.

Sources: R3, R4, R7 [1:52, 1:58, 1:70]; PF-15 coherence bias, PF-16 matched baseline, PF-18
Hilbert on short windows, PF-19 window-level permutations, PF-20 collapsed criteria, PF-21
circular responders, PF-25 directed in undirected, PF-26 missing channels [2]; P13 tasks 1–3
and P18 [3]; L17 [7]. *To open:* Lachaux 1999 (PLV); Vinck et al. 2011 (wPLI); Nolte 2004
(imaginary coherency); Zalesky et al. 2010 (NBS); Benjamini & Hochberg 1995; Maris &
Oostenveld 2007 (cluster permutation).

### G21. Signal processing on continuous data; metadata from the file

> Filter continuous recordings, then cut segments. Segments within the filter's ringing time
> of a discontinuity or artifact are discarded or flagged. The sampling rate, channel names
> and units come from the file header, never from a constant. Channels are selected by name,
> never by position.

Sources: R3 [1:52], PF-14 edge effects, PF-04 wrong sampling rate, PF-05/PF-06 channels by
position [2]. Partly covered by G5 (explicit parameters).

### G22. Long and GPU jobs

> Every job in a plan states its device, expected runtime, seeds and output folder.
> - **Who runs it.** The orchestrator runs CPU jobs under `LOCAL_CPU_MIN` (default 10) and
>   GPU jobs under `LOCAL_GPU_MIN` (default 0 = never) after the audit PASS. Anything longer,
>   or on remote hardware (Colab, cluster), goes to the supervisor as an exact command, and the
>   run waits for its outputs.
> - **Smoke test first.** Before a long job, a committed smoke test (synthetic data or one
>   fold/participant, under 5 minutes, CPU) runs the same entry point end to end.
> - **Resumability.** Long jobs save each fold with its configuration. A rerun with a
>   different configuration on the same output folder is refused.
> - **No background work.** Agents never leave un-awaited background jobs (L23).
> - **Reporting.** Runtime and device are reported per job.
> - **Limits.** The limits are set by the supervisor, per project, in one committed file
>   (`docs/COMPUTE.md` or a section of the config).

Sources: `LOCAL_RUN_BUDGET_MIN: 120`, `MAX_GPU_HOURS_PER_PHASE: 2` [5 config.yaml:21–22];
verifier step 4, the human-run request [4 PROCEDURES]; PF-37 mixed configurations, PF-40
Colab [2]; P21 and P23 resumable folds and smoke test [3]; L23 [7]; the current rule
[9:171].

### G23. Derivations go through committed, tested tools (L27)

> Any number derived for a handoff or write-up comes from a committed tool with a test
> before it enters the handoff. A handoff that cites a path outside the repository cannot
> be READY. The auditor checks both.

Sources: L27, L4 [7]; `BACKLOG.md` [8]. Extends G11.

### Amendment to G2 (tests)

> A known-answer test for a fix or a layout (reshape, flatten, coefficient slicing,
> adjacency) must fail on the plausible buggy variant: wrong axis, contiguous vs strided,
> cross-unit, leaked fit, no-op. The test file shows that it does.

Sources: R6 [1:66], PF-17 [2], synthetic-tester step 3 [4 PROCEDURES].

## 3. How the rules are carried (agents, skills, tools)

**Agents.** The six agents stay. The SSVEP project's 13 roles [4] map onto them:

| SSVEP role | Harness agent |
|---|---|
| Classical ML, Deep Learning, Connectivity & Stats, Data Engineer, Signal Processing, Online | pipeline-engineer |
| Evaluation Lead, Scientific Reviewer | methods-auditor (plan audit + code audit) |
| Verifier, Runner | orchestrator's real runs + `compare_runs` |
| Scribe | results-analyst |

I recommend **method skills** instead of new agents. The engineer, tester and auditor
load them when the plan uses the method:
- `.claude/skills/deep-learning/`: G17, G18 and G19 as a checklist, the per-seed output
  schema, train accuracy and parameter count;
- `.claude/skills/connectivity/`: G20 as a checklist, with the required known-answer
  tests (bias on noise, edge immunity, planted component found by NBS, FPR on noise);
- `.claude/skills/compute-jobs/`: G22, the human-run request format, and resumable folds.

This keeps the agents' context small when a project uses only classical ML.
The auditor's templates (`02b_plan_audit.md`, `05_audit.md`) get rows for G17–G23.

**Vendored tools** (`eegharness/`, general, each with known-answer and FPR tests):

| Tool | Content | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| `splits` | grouped splitters, inner grouped validation, `assert_disjoint(train, test, groups)`, group ids unique across participants | G1, G13, G17 |
| `permutation` | unit-level permutation with observed group sizes; p including the observed labelling | G13, G20d |
| `multiple` | BH-FDR; NBS (pseudo-z, components, max-size null, threshold sweep) | G20e |
| `seeds` | seed everything (numpy, random, torch if installed, DataLoader generator); device and determinism provenance | G18 |
| `folds` | resumable fold store that saves the configuration and refuses mismatches | G22 |
| `chance` | binomial chance level for n test trials and k classes | G19 |

The estimators themselves (PLV, wPLI, coherence, EEGNet, GNNs) stay **project code**,
built per plan with the tests the skills require. The kit gives the statistical and
evaluation scaffolding, not the methods. torch stays optional: `eegharness` must import
without it.

**Guard / auditor checks** (proposed, to be decided):
- an auditor check that no handoff cites a path outside the repository (G23);
- an auditor check that every reported NN number has ≥ N seeds in its output (G18);
- a guard block on writing or running scripts in `/tmp` / `$TMPDIR` (G23). Possible false
  blocks need checking, since the kit tests themselves use temporary folders.
